COMMAND INBOX · UX REVIEW 2 SEP 2026

The structure is right. The decision moment was underserved.

An expert review of the support-automation prototype against published human-AI interaction guidance — Google PAIR, Microsoft's CHI 2019 guidelines, Apple's HIG — and empirical work on confidence display and approval workflows from 2020–2026. Five findings were fixed in the prototype this pass; five are on the watchlist.

Method: heuristic expert review, no users. Treat severity as hypothesis — a 5–8 participant task study on the approve/reject flow would settle the open calls.

WHAT HOLDS UP
The three lanes are the textbook gate model. Auto / Draft / You maps one-to-one onto the auto-approve, soft-gate, hard-gate tiers recommended for agent systems (Hitchhiker's Guide to Agentic AI). Money movement hard-gates behind maker-checker; drafts soft-gate behind one approver.
Low confidence changes behavior, not just color. Below the 0.78 bar the AI stands down or flags the paragraph — exactly the "prompt the user to check in low-confidence, high-stakes situations" pattern (PAIR), and consistent with evidence that low displayed confidence pushes people back onto their own judgment (CDSS study, 2025).
Drafts are grounded and admit gaps. Cited sources only, and the trade-finance draft says in writing that no approved position exists — curated evidence over chain-of-thought dumps (agent UX patterns).
Accountability is explicit. Sign-in states that approvals are recorded against your name; every run ends in the audit log — the separation-of-duties value HITL reviews exist for (StackAI).
FIXED THIS PASS — FIVE FINDINGS
01
The approve bar told you who signs — not what you're signing up for

An approval gate should show what runs, what changes, and how to undo it (gate design principles). Ours showed the approver chain and a button. Severity: high — this is the product's core interaction.

Changed — the gateway now states reversibility at the point of commitment: "Cannot be undone" on money movement, "Recallable for 60s" on replies.
02
Customer wait time was invisible at the decision point

Queue guidance is unambiguous: countdowns belong where people act, so they move before a breach, not after (Featurebase, Titanapps). The list showed deadline chips; the approve bar showed nothing.

Changed — "Customer waiting 2h 48m" now sits beside the Approve button, colored by deadline pressure.
03
Confidence spoke only in decimals

Qualitative labels beat raw scores for trust calibration — technical formats read as noise to non-specialists (MIT CSAIL 2024 / NN/g 2023); framing matters as much as the number (Zhang et al., FAT* 2020). "0.94" carried no verdict.

Changed — every confidence readout now leads with the word: "High · 0.94", "classified with high confidence (0.94)". The trace keeps raw numbers for engineers.
04
Routing was silent about its reasons

Skill- and load-based routing is standard (Supportbench), and agent systems should "show why routing happened" (HatchWorks) — the make-clear-why guideline from CHI 2019. Staff trust assignment they can see the logic of.

Changed — the assignment event now reads "Assigned to P. Sharma — closest skill match for disputed transactions, lightest live load in Cards."
05
No duplicate-execution check where money moves

Retries and re-approvals make one approved action execute twice; the fix is idempotency keys plus execution-time checks that the action hasn't already occurred (StackAI). The approver had no way to see that check.

Changed — irreversible actions now show "✓ No similar action on this account in 90 days" at the gate.
WATCHLIST — NOT SHIPPED, WORTH TESTING
Gate volume. If the inbox gets noisy, reviewers rubber-stamp — the failure mode approval queues die of (StackAI). Track approve-without-open rate as the canary metric.
Batch approvals. Related pending actions should present together, not one by one (gate principles) — pairs with the team lead's missing bulk actions.
Draft edits as eval data. When staff rewrite a draft before sending, the diff is the cheapest training signal the AI screens never see — the feedback-loop chapter of PAIR.
Overconfidence drift. Displayed confidence must keep matching observed accuracy or trust collapses (miscalibration study, 2024) — surface calibration on the agents screen, not just eval scores.
Global search. ⌘K jumps tickets; it should reach customers, knowledge and policies from the same box.
